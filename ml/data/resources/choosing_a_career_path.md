# Choosing a Career Path

Choosing a career is easier when you treat it as a series of experiments rather than one big decision. Start with what you already know about yourself: the subjects and activities you enjoy, the skills people say you are good at, and the kind of environment you like, such as offices, field work, labs or working with people.

Use the career assessment in this app to get a first list of roles and career areas, then read the career pages to see the day-to-day tasks and skills. A role is worth exploring further if the tasks sound interesting, not only the title or the pay.

Test your ideas cheaply. Talk to someone who does the job, shadow them for a day, take a short online course, or choose an attachment in that field. After each experience ask yourself what you enjoyed, what drained you and what you want to learn next.

Remember that early careers are rarely straight lines. Many people move between related roles as they discover what suits them, and skills such as communication, problem solving and digital literacy carry across fields.

When you are unsure, speak to your university career office, a lecturer or a mentor. They can help you weigh options and plan the next practical step.
