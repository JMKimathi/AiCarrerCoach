# Online and Freelance Work

Online and freelance work can help you earn while studying, build a portfolio and gain experience before a full-time job. Common areas include writing and editing, graphic design, web and app development, data entry and annotation, transcription, virtual assistance and digital marketing.

Start with one skill you can already do reasonably well and build three to five samples you can show clients. A simple portfolio page or a shared folder is enough at first.

Use established platforms and keep all agreements and payments on the platform, which protects you if a client does not pay. Read each platform's rules, fees and payment methods before you begin. The government's Ajira Digital programme also offers training and guidance for young people starting online work.

Be careful with offers that ask you to pay for an account, buy "training" before you can earn, or move payment outside the platform. These are common scams.

Treat freelancing like a small business. Agree the task, deadline and price in writing, deliver on time, and keep records of what you earn. Income from freelancing may be taxable, so check your obligations with the Kenya Revenue Authority.
