# Writing a CV for Any Field

For a student or recent graduate, a CV should fit on one or two pages. Put the most relevant information first: a short profile, education, skills, experience (including attachments, volunteering and projects), and referees.

Write a three-line profile that names the kind of role you want and what you bring, for example: "Final-year BCom Finance student with attachment experience in a SACCO, comfortable with Excel and customer service, seeking a graduate role in banking."

Under experience, list what you did and what came of it, starting each line with an action word: prepared, supported, organised, analysed, taught. Add numbers where they are true: "served about 40 members a day", "reconciled weekly cash reports".

List skills that match the advert, and be ready to give an example of each in an interview. Leave out photographs, your ID number and your date of birth unless the advert asks for them.

Tailor the CV for every application. Read the advert, underline the key requirements, and make sure each one appears in your CV where it is true. Save the file as a PDF with your name in the file name.
