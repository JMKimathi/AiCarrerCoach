# Using LinkedIn and Networking to Find Opportunities

Many internships and graduate roles are filled through contacts before or instead of being advertised. A good LinkedIn profile and a few genuine conversations can open doors.

Use a clear, professional photo and a headline that says what you are working towards, such as "Agriculture student | Interested in agronomy and agribusiness". In the About section write three or four sentences about your interests and what you are learning. Add your attachment, projects and volunteering under Experience.

Follow organisations you would like to work for and turn on job alerts for your field. Connect with lecturers, classmates, alumni and people you meet at career fairs, adding a short note to say how you know them.

Ask for informational conversations rather than jobs. A message such as "I am a second-year nursing student and would value ten minutes to hear how you started in community health" is easy to say yes to. Prepare three questions, keep to time, and thank the person afterwards.

Share your work occasionally: a project, something you learnt on attachment or an event you attended. Keep posts professional and positive.
