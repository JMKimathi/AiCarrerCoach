# Understanding Pay and Job Offers

Salary figures in adverts and surveys are usually gross pay, meaning pay before deductions. Your take-home (net) pay is lower because employers deduct PAYE income tax, NSSF pension contributions, the Social Health Insurance Fund (SHIF) contribution and the Affordable Housing Levy. The rates change from time to time, so check the current figures on the Kenya Revenue Authority website or ask the employer's HR office for a payslip example.

Pay varies a lot between employers, sectors and towns. Salary surveys such as Paylab Kenya give a typical range for each field: starting salaries usually sit near the lower end and experienced staff nearer the upper end. Use these ranges to judge whether an offer is reasonable, not as a promise of what you will earn.

Internship stipends are not salaries. Public service internships pay a monthly stipend set by government, while private internships range from well paid to unpaid. Always check the stipend in the advert before you apply.

When you receive an offer, ask for it in writing. Check the job title, start date, gross pay, allowances, probation period, working hours, leave days and notice period. It is acceptable to ask questions before you sign, and for a first job it is usually wiser to ask about training and growth than to negotiate hard on pay.
