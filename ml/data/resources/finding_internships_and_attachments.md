# Finding Internships and Industrial Attachments in Kenya

Start looking at least three months before your attachment or internship is due. Many organisations fill places early, and university placement offices often have a deadline for approval letters.

Look in several places at once. Your university career office usually knows which employers took students before. General job boards such as BrighterMonday, Fuzu, MyJobMag and LinkedIn list private-sector internships. For government, the Public Service Commission runs the Public Service Internship Programme, a 12-month paid placement for degree holders that is advertised once a year on the PSC jobs portal. County public service boards advertise their own internships on county websites.

Do not wait only for adverts. Send a short email to organisations you would like to join: say who you are, what you study, the dates you are available and one thing you can already do for them. Attach a one-page CV and your university introduction letter.

Keep a simple tracker with the organisation, the date you applied, the contact person and when to follow up. Follow up politely after about two weeks.

Treat an attachment as a long interview. Arrive on time, keep your logbook up to date, ask for real tasks, and ask your supervisor for a reference before you leave.
